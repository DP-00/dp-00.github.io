Why VSETH should champion a climate-friendly and plant-based catering

Plant-based is the single most important factor

Minimal land use

A thousand years ago, less than 4% of habitable land was used for farming. Today it’s 44%. Only a quarter grows food eaten directly by people, while 70% supports livestock that provide just 38% of global protein and 17% of calories. If everyone ate a vegan diet, farmland use could fall by 75%, freeing an area equal to North America and Brazil combined.

Lower water use

70% of global freshwater is used for agriculture. Animal products require vastly more resources: for example, producing 1 kg of beef requires around 15,000 liters of water, while the same amount of lentils requires only about 1,250 liters—12 times less!

Greenhouse Gas Emissions

The global food system is responsible for around one third of worldwide greenhouse gas emissions. Most come from land-use change and livestock. Research from Oxford University shows that switching to a fully plant-based diet could cut food-related emissions by up to 73%.

Biodiversity

Extensive land use for grazing and feed crops is a major cause of deforestation and species loss. Today, 70% of all bird biomass consists of domestic birds. Among mammals, livestock make up 62%, humans 34%, and only 4%, from mice to whales, are wild.

Plant-based food is economically sensible

It's just cheaper

While it's true that meat-substitute products can cost more than meat, designing a plant-based diet intentionally can reduce costs. The Bryant report (2024) gives an example of this, estimating that plant-based meals are on average “on average 30% cheaper than meat-based meals, and 21% cheaper than vegetarian meals”. We can understand this intuitively: plant-based food is simply cheaper to produce, animal products are only kept afloat by large government subsidies. Thus, plant-based can prevent further cost increases in the ETH mensas.

Fostering innovation

As climate change starts impacting everyday life more and more, the need to adapt will become increasingly pressing. Plant-based food is one part of the picture and we can stay ahead of the curve by supporting this transition. The ETH spin-off Planted focusing on meat substitutes is one of many Swiss start-ups in this space, but other technologies like precision fermentation, vegan dairy or cultured meat are promising as well.

A proven model

Whether in Zürich or abroad, fully plant-based mensas already exist and thrive. Furthermore, many canteens choose to increase the ratio of plant-based food in their meals. While the plant-based model is far from ubiquitous due to cultural reasons, it is demonstrably viable and gaining ground every year.

Plant-based food has all you need

Proteins

Among all the existing sources of dietary proteins, plant-based sources dominate the supply of proteins with 57 %. And for a good reason: even animal proteins ultimately come from a plant source.

The 9 essential amino acids can be obtained from combined out of different plant-based meals a day. Nonetheless there are plant foods like soy and quinoa that contain all essential amino acids by themselves.

Nutrients

Evidence indicates that plant-based dietary patterns are associated with health and environmental benefits. Even many athletes prefer a plant-based diet and achieve good results thanks to it.

Fewer Allergies

Plant-based diets were consistently associated with a lower incidence of allergic sensitization and reduced symptom severity. Of course, mensas still need to communicate with students and make sure nobody is left behind.

Out of the most common food allergies, lactose, egg and fish are not relevant as plant-based meals do not contain them naturally. However nut and gluten allergies must be considered.

Reduction in Diseases

Plant-based diets can lower risk of chronic diseases, cancers, and asthma.

They also support healthy weight and gut microbiome.
